Why Did Jesus Call God 'Father' and Himself 'Son'?

Short Answer: Jesus called God “Father” and referred to Himself as “Son” to show their relationship within the one divine being. This does not mean they are two gods — it means that within the one God, there are distinct persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

🔹 What the Bible Shows

1. One God, Not Many:
Deuteronomy 6:4 — “Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one.”
Isaiah 45:5 — “I am the Lord, and there is no other; there is no God besides Me.”

2. Yet Jesus Calls God His Father:
John 5:17–18 — Jesus said, “My Father is working until now, and I am working.” The Jews understood He was making Himself equal with God.

3. The Son Is Fully God:
John 1:1 — “In the beginning was the Word... and the Word was God.”
John 10:30 — Jesus said, “I and the Father are one”
Colossians 2:9 — “In Christ dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily.”

4. But the Son Is Not the Father:
John 14:28 — “The Father is greater than I.” (This speaks of role, not nature.)
Jesus the Son obeyed the Father — not because He is less divine — but because He humbled Himself to accomplish salvation (Philippians 2:6–8). So, Jesus Christ coming down in down on fresh was the act of Obedience and humbleness. so, the Father on the throne of heaven is at that moment Greater than him by role but in nature Its one and one true God That's why before the ascension, Jesus was a son of God, but after He ascended, He is now God the Son and our Lord.

🔹 Understanding the Trinity (in simple terms)

The Bible reveals that:

  • 📌 There is one God (not three gods)
  • 📌 This one God exists as three persons: the Father, the Son (Jesus), and the Holy Spirit
  • 📌 Each person is fully God, yet the same as the other, but different in Role.

This is called the doctrine of the Trinity. It’s not a contradiction — it's a mystery of God's divine nature that He reveals in Scripture.

Think of the sun: it gives off light, heat, and exists as a star. Three forms — one source. Or water: it can be ice, liquid, or vapor — still H2O. These are limited human analogies for something far greater — God's triune nature.

🔹 Why This Matters

Jesus being the “Son” shows that He is eternally loved, sent, and honored by the Father (John 3:16, John 17:5). Jesus did not become the Son when He was born — He was always the Son, and always fully God.

So no, Jesus calling God ‘Father’ doesn’t mean they are two separate deities. Instead, it reflects the divine relationship within the one True God — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — working together to save us. ❓ Why Was Jesus Rejected by His Own People but First Worshiped by Foreigners?

John 1:11 — “He came unto His own, and His own received Him not.”

Though Jesus was born a Jew, raised in Jewish culture, and fulfilled Jewish prophecy, **many of His own people — including leaders like King Herod and the Pharisees — rejected Him**. Meanwhile, Gentile foreigners, known as the **Magi or Wise Men**, came from the East to worship Him at His birth (Matthew 2:1–12).

🔍 Why Did This Happen?

1. The Jews expected a military Messiah, not a humble child.
Many in Israel were hoping for a political deliverer to free them from Roman rule. Jesus came instead as a suffering servant (Isaiah ), born in a manger — not what they expected.

2. Pride and spiritual blindness hardened their hearts.
Jesus confronted their hypocrisy and called for true repentance. Many rejected Him because they loved tradition more than truth (Mark 7:6–9).

3. God was revealing a bigger plan — salvation for all people.
The Magi’s visit fulfilled prophecies showing that even **Gentiles** would seek the Messiah. This revealed that Jesus wasn’t just for the Jews, but for **the whole world** (Isaiah 49:6).

👑 Who Were the Magi?

The Magi (Matthew 2:1–12) were likely **Persian or Babylonian wise men**, possibly influenced by the Jewish prophecies Daniel left behind during the exile (Daniel 2:48). They were astrologers and scholars who studied the stars and ancient scrolls. When they saw a unique star (Numbers 24:17), they followed it to find the “King of the Jews.”

🎁 What Gifts Did They Bring — And Why?

  • Gold — fit for a king (symbol of Christ’s royalty)
  • Frankincense — used in worship (symbol of His divinity; Exodus 30:34)
  • Myrrh — used in burial (points to His death and sacrifice; John 19:39)

Each gift was **prophetic**: Jesus is our King, our God, and our Sacrificial Savior.

📖 What Does This Teach Us Today?

  • 🌍 God's salvation is for **everyone** — not just one nation, culture, or race.
  • ❤️ Jesus welcomes **anyone** who seeks Him with sincerity — even outsiders.
  • 📜 The religious and proud may miss Him, while the humble and hungry will find Him.
  • 🔔 We are called to worship like the Magi — bringing our best to Christ in faith.

Qn7:

🤔 Was Evil/Sin Created by God?

This is a deep and honest question: Did God create evil? The short answer is: No, God did not create evil/sin in the same way He created light, the earth, or humans. Rather, evil is the result of the rejection of good, just like darkness is the absence of light or cold is the absence of heat.

In Genesis 1:31, after creating everything, "God saw all that He had made, and it was very good." There was no mention of evil in creation — only goodness and order.

Isaiah 45:7 (KJV) says: “I form the light, and create darkness: I make peace, and create evil: I the Lord do all these things.”
Explanation: The word "evil" here refers to calamity or judgment (like war or disaster), not moral wickedness. In Hebrew, the word ra can mean trouble, disaster, or harm — not always sin.

So where did moral evil come from? The answer lies in free will. God created beings (like angels and humans) with the freedom to choose. When Satan (Lucifer) chose pride over worship, evil was born through that rebellion (Isaiah 14:12–14, Ezekiel 28:15).

Similarly, humans brought evil into the world by disobeying God in the Garden of Eden. This is why we read in Romans 5:12: “Sin entered the world through one man, and death through sin...”

💡 Example to Understand: Imagine a room filled with light. When someone blocks the light, shadows appear. The shadow wasn’t created — it’s the result of blocking the light. In the same way, evil is the result of choosing against God’s goodness.

In short: God created free beings with the capacity to love Him by choice. Evil arose when that freedom was used to reject Him.

If Jesus is really God and He died for our sins, how is this possible?

Users often wonder: If Jesus is God, and He died on the cross, does it mean God can die? Who ruled the world during His death? How does this mystery work?

✔ Understanding the Mystery

The death of Jesus does NOT mean God ceased to exist. Jesus has two natures: fully God and fully man (John 1:14, Philippians 2:6-8). His human body truly died, but His divine nature remained alive and sovereign. God cannot die in His divine essence (Malachi 3:6, “I the Lord do not change”).

✔ Who ruled when Jesus died?

God remained on His throne. Jesus said, John 10:30 – “I and the Father are one.” The Father never stopped being God. The Son’s divine nature never ceased. This is God’s sovereign capability: to be present in heaven and on earth at the same time (Psalm 139:7-8, “Where can I go from your Spirit?”).

✔ Why did Jesus die physically?

Jesus died to pay the penalty for sin because “the wages of sin is death” (Romans 6:23). In human form, He took our punishment, satisfying God’s justice (Isaiah 53:5-6, Hebrews 9:22).

It's God on the throne of heaven ,(father) destroying himself who is in the fresh (Jesus.)

✔ What was He doing in death?

While His body lay in the tomb, His spirit went to proclaim victory over the powers of darkness (1 Peter 3:18-19). He also opened the way for the righteous dead to enter God’s presence (Ephesians 4:8-10).

✔ How could God still rule?

God is omnipresent and all-powerful. Even as Jesus experienced death in His humanity, His divinity remained active, sustaining the universe (Colossians 1:17, “In Him all things hold together”).

This mystery shows God’s love and power: He humbled Himself to save us, yet never ceased to be God.
